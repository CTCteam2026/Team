What stands out

The service charge is the single biggest driver

Blackhawk applies 35%, Ruby Hill 24%. At Ruby Hill's rate, Blackhawk's total would fall by $4,281. The lower Danville sales tax (8.75% vs 10.25%) offsets only a small part of it. 35% sits above the 20–25% market norm — worth asking whether it is negotiable.

Blackhawk's food bill will move — Ruby Hill's won't

Blackhawk prices each entrée separately from $33 to $85, so the final number depends on what guests choose. The quote assumes 90 filet / 50 salmon / 50 chicken / 10 vegetarian; a filet-heavy room would add roughly $4,800. Ruby Hill stays at $70 regardless — the dinner cost is fixed the day you sign.

Dessert is included at Ruby Hill and extra at Blackhawk

Ruby Hill's $70 covers salad, starch, vegetable, dessert and coffee. Blackhawk includes salad, bread, coffee and menu cards, but the dessert bar is $15 per guest — $3,000 on top. That is most of the $27 gap on the dinner line.

The champagne greet is Blackhawk's, and it's free

200 glasses passed on arrival at no charge — comparable greets are usually billed at $8–12 per guest. Ruby Hill pours house champagne at the bar in every package but offers no passed greet. Worth asking Eve for one: the champagne is already in the package, so it should cost little.

Two quantities to confirm at Blackhawk

The cheese platters are priced per 50 people and two are on the order — coverage for 100 of 200 guests. Two more is about $880 all-in. Passed appetizers run 3.5 pieces per guest, where 4–6 is standard for a full cocktail hour.

The bars aren't comparable, and neither total includes A/V

Blackhawk's beer & wine covers all four hours; Ruby Hill's premium bar covers three of its five and needs about $7,655 more for full coverage — still leaving it roughly $9,800 cheaper. Budget $2,000–6,000 for A/V either way; Blackhawk requires you to use its provider (OAVS) directly. Ruby Hill's quote expires 29 August.
