# Priority Rules

Apply the three tiers **in order**. Tier 1 beats Tier 2 beats Tier 3, always. A cold vendor email about an unpaid deposit outranks a friendly client note about an event next week.

## Tier 1 — Money & commitment risk (dominant)

Anything in this list goes to the top regardless of client, event date, or sender:

- Contracts, agreements, addenda, redlines, anything awaiting a signature
- Invoices, deposits, payment failures, late notices, refunds, chargebacks
- Cancellations, postponements, date changes, venue changes
- Change orders, scope changes, or any request that alters what CTC owes
- Any dollar figure paired with a deadline
- Attrition, minimums, force majeure, or penalty language
- Insurance certificates, W-9s, vendor onboarding blocking a payment
- A client expressing dissatisfaction, frustration, or escalating

**Within Tier 1**, order by dollar amount if stated, then by deadline, then by event proximity.

## Tier 2 — Who is waiting on you

An explicit ask directed at the reader, weighted by age:

| Age of the unanswered ask | Weight |
|---|---|
| > 72 hours | Highest |
| 48–72 hours | High |
| 24–48 hours | Medium |
| < 24 hours | Normal |

Signals that something is a real ask: a question mark addressed to the reader, "can you," "please confirm," "let me know by," "waiting on," "following up," "checking in," "second request," or a forwarded thread with no comment (someone is handing it to you).

**A follow-up on a thread the reader never answered is automatically Tier 2 High**, even if the original was low-stakes. That is the dropped-ball detector, and it is the single highest-value thing this report does.

Weight *up* if the sender is a client or the venue for an event inside 30 days. Weight *down* if the sender is a prospect or cold.

## Tier 3 — Event date proximity

Tiebreaker. Nearest event first, using the `MM-DD` prefix on the ClickUp folder name.

| Days to event | Weight |
|---|---|
| ≤ 14 days | Critical — treat routine questions as urgent |
| 15–45 days | High |
| 46–90 days | Normal |
| > 90 days | Low |
| No event / internal | Lowest |

Inside 14 days of an event, the tolerance for a slow answer is near zero. Anything touching a load-in, headcount, room block, F&B guarantee, or run-of-show for an event that close should surface even if it reads casually.

## Assigning labels

- **P1 — needs you today.** Tier 1 items, plus Tier 2 High on a client or an event inside 30 days. **Hard cap of 8.** If more qualify, take the top 8 and drop the rest to P2, and say in the report that the cap was hit.
- **P2 — this week.** Everything else actionable.
- **P3 / FYI.** Informational, no reply expected, worth knowing.
- **Suppressed.** Newsletters, subscriptions, automated notifications, cold outreach. Listed as a count and sender names only — never summarized.

## Deliberately *not* priority signals

Ignore these; they are noise dressed up as urgency:

- The sender marking it high-importance or putting URGENT in the subject
- Exclamation points, all caps, red flags
- Seniority of the sender
- Length of the email

Cold outreach in particular is engineered to look urgent. Judge on the three tiers only.
