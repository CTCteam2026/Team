The 2027 rate decision

Three things to do, in order, and what each one unblocks. The first costs nothing and settles whether the rest are urgent. Do not change a client rate until step 1 is done.

Why $90 an hour is the question

A billed hour has to carry four things before a dollar of profit: the salary, the payroll burden on it, the share of the year that person is not billable, and the overhead that keeps the lights on. Here is a Southern California Event Manager at 80% utilization and 15% overhead.

What to do

1

Measure two numbers

This month · from records you already have · no client impact

Utilization. For each staff member over the last 12 months: hours charged to a client project ÷ total hours paid. ClickUp time entries against payroll.

Overhead rate. Total operating expense that is not project labor — rent, software, insurance, phones, admin and BD salaries, professional fees — divided by total project labor cost. One number from the P&L.

Everything below is conditional on these two. They take a day and they decide whether this is an urgent repricing or a minor tune-up.

2

Re-cost the P&L on the real basis

Same week · a change to the builder · still no client impact

Two edits to the model:

  • Cost rate becomes salary + burden ÷ billable hours, not ÷ 2,080. The builder gets a utilization input; $60 and $42 stop being typed in and start being derived.
  • The $0.61/hr overhead line becomes a percentage of direct labor — the number from step 1. At 15% that is roughly $9 on a manager hour, not 61 cents.

Then re-run the last four P&Ls and read the real margins. Rename the current "% Profit" column to "% Gross Margin" whatever happens — it has never been net profit, and treating it as such is how we got here.

3

Reprice on tiers, not two rates

Next proposal cycle · client-facing

Today a Senior Event Manager and an Event Manager both bill $90, and every coordinator bills $65. Senior hours cost $109 and bill the same as hours costing $88 — our most experienced people are our least profitable. Five tiers fixes that without any single rate moving as far.

If a 40–50% increase is not sellable

Margin is a ratio. Rates are one lever on it and usually the hardest one to pull on an existing client. These move the same number.

Lever

Phase it over the renewal cycle

Half the gap in 2027, half in 2028. New logos get the full card immediately, so the increase never has to be explained to someone who already signed.

Lever

Sell fewer hours at a higher rate

The scale index already stops hours ballooning with attendance. Trimming 15% of pre-planning hours and raising rates 20% lands the same revenue at a better margin, and reads as a leaner proposal.

Lever

Make the admin fee real

2% returns about one margin point. A 6–8% administrative fee, or a flat project management fee, is normal in this industry and does not touch the hourly rates clients compare.

Lever

Stop discounting off the top

The 5% multi-year discount comes straight out of net. At a true 10% margin it is half the profit. Tie it to something that lowers our cost — a signed multi-year term, earlier commitment, fewer site visits.

What not to do

Do not cut pay. $60 and $42 imply $96,000 and $67,200 salaries, both at market for Southern California. Paying under market on roles this hard to hire is a false economy, and it is not where the gap is.

Do not reprice before step 1. If utilization turns out to be 95%, the proposed card is roughly 25% too high and we would be walking into renewals with a number we cannot defend.

Do not hire the Admin Assistant as overhead. At $53/hr true cost the role only works if the hours are billable — the proposal template already has Document Management and Registration admin lines that could carry them. Decide that before the offer, not after.

The likely reality. CTC is a going concern with a healthy book, so utilization is probably well above 75% and overhead below 20% — the top half of that table, not the bottom. The point is not that the business is broken. It is that we have been quoting gross margin as if it were profit, and nobody knows by how much. One day of timesheet work closes that.

Basis. Salaries are 2027 Southern California midpoints from the rate benchmark, payroll burden at 30%, and the utilization and overhead figures stated on each table. True cost = (salary × 1.30 ÷ utilization) × (1 + overhead). Proposed rate = true cost ÷ (1 − target margin).

Confidence. The salary inputs are aggregator medians and vary widely by source. Utilization and overhead are assumptions, not measurements — that is the entire reason step 1 comes first. Treat every rate here as a starting point for the conversation, not a decision already made.
